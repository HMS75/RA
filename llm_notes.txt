QUESTION: What methods reduce LLM hallucination?
======================================================================
To reduce LLM hallucinations, researchers employ mitigation and prevention strategies applied at the data, training, and inference stages of the LLM lifecycle [1][2]. Specific techniques include Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to ground outputs using external documents, and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) to penalize factually incorrect claims [3]. Other approaches involve knowledge-based methods like knowledge graphs, training or reference guiding via control codes or contrastive learning, and evaluating or correcting generated content (such as rectifying self-contradictory statements) [4][5]. Finally, methods such as fine-tuning, memory augmentation, context prompts, clarifying question frameworks, grammar-guided decoding, and specialized grounding models like RHO also help mitigate hallucinations [4][5][6].

QUESTION: What are the limitations of retrieval-augmented generation?
======================================================================
Based on the provided sources, the limitations of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) include:

* **Multi-turn dialogue constraints:** RAG methods have limitations when applied directly to multi-turn dialogue contexts, despite being effective in open-domain question answering [1].
* **Error accumulation:** RAG approaches occasionally face the challenge of error accumulation, where irrelevant evidence can seep into the generation process [4]. 
* **External hallucination:** This leakage of irrelevant retrieved information can lead to incorrect responses, a phenomenon known as external hallucination [4].
